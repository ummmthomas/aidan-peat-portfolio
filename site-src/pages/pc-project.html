Four years taking one artist's lobster from the canvas to whisky, sneakers, laptops and city streets.

Born in Scotland and living and working in London, Colbert is often referred to as the "godson of Andy Warhol". He has created a global following for his cartoon lobster persona and his hyper-pop history paintings. During my time with Colbert, I've worked on a wide range of projects: high-end collectable artworks, documentaries, global exhibition advertisements, paintings, books, and football stadium animations.

Why the lobster travels

Colbert's lobster is a character, not a logo, so it can move into other people's worlds without losing itself. It sits on a full moon for Johnnie Walker, wears the AS Roma kit, covers a Bentley and fronts The Big Issue. The work on these pages is about keeping that character unmistakably Colbert while letting each partner's own world show through.

Colbert's work has received international acclaim in museums and galleries worldwide for his energetic new approach to painting and pop theory. Following on from early Pop painters such as Richard Hamilton, Roy Lichtenstein and James Rosenquist, Colbert's paintings cross high art themes from old master paintings and contemporary art theory with everyday symbols of mass contemporary culture, all narrated through the eyes of Colbert's cartoon Lobster alter ego. He has been championed as a contemporary pop master by art world figures such as Charles Saatchi & Simon de Pury.
